# OLIPOP — ICP Cards

Source: drinkolipop.com positioning/testimonials + inference from product design (flagged where inferred, not sourced verbatim).

## Card 1 — The Soda-Nostalgic Health Switcher
**Who:** Adult who grew up drinking classic soda (root beer, cola, cream soda), now health-conscious but doesn't want to give up the ritual/taste.
**Pain points:** Guilt around sugar/calorie intake from regular soda; diet soda alternatives (artificial sweeteners) taste bad or feel worse; wants a treat that doesn't derail health goals.
**Voice-of-customer phrases (sourced):** "It's gently sweet and creamy, and almost drinks like a dessert." "All the flavors are wonderful and sweet, despite only sporting 35–45 calories per can."
**Objections:** "Does it actually taste like real soda or like a health-food knockoff?" "Is the sugar reduction from weird artificial sweeteners?"
**What lands:** Exact sugar/calorie numbers, real-ingredient list, nostalgic flavor names (Vintage Cola, Classic Root Beer).

## Card 2 — The Gut-Health / Digestive Wellness Seeker
**Who:** Actively managing digestion, bloating, or general gut health; likely already takes fiber supplements, probiotics, or follows gut-health content online. *(Inferred from prebiotic/fiber-forward product design and Purdue research citation — not a directly quoted persona.)*
**Pain points:** Low fiber intake from typical diet; existing fiber supplements are chalky/unpleasant; wants function without it feeling like "medicine."
**Voice-of-customer phrases:** N/A directly sourced — use functional facts as substitute hook: "Excellent Source of Fiber," "9g fiber per can."
**Objections:** "Is prebiotic fiber actually going to do anything, or is this marketing?" "Will it cause bloating/gas like other fiber products?"
**What lands:** Purdue University research citation, specific fiber sources (chicory root, cassava root), FDA "Excellent Source of Fiber" labeling fact — avoid disease-claim language, stick to structure/function framing.

## Card 3 — The Parent Choosing Better-For-Kids Options
**Who:** Parent looking for a soda-like treat to offer kids that's lower in sugar than conventional soda. *(Inferred from FAQ addressing children directly.)*
**Pain points:** Wants to say yes to a treat without full-sugar soda guilt; needs reassurance on caffeine content and general safety for kids.
**Voice-of-customer phrases:** N/A directly sourced.
**Objections:** "Is this safe for my kid?" "Which flavors have caffeine so I know which to avoid?"
**What lands:** Caffeine-free flavor callouts, low sugar number, "consult your pediatrician if underlying conditions" disclaimer used honestly (never suppressed).
**Compliance note:** Any ad targeting this segment must avoid implying medical clearance for children — mirror the FAQ's own hedge language.

## Card 4 — The Label-Reader / Clean-Ingredient Shopper
**Who:** Reads ingredient panels, avoids artificial sweeteners/colors/flavors, shops Whole Foods/Target better-for-you aisles. *(Inferred from ingredient-forward site content and non-GMO/vegan/gluten-free/paleo badges.)*
**Pain points:** Distrust of "healthy" marketing that hides synthetic ingredients; wants recognizable, plant-based ingredient names.
**Voice-of-customer phrases:** N/A directly sourced.
**Objections:** "What's actually sweetening this — real stevia or a blend I can't pronounce?" "Is 'natural flavors' hiding something?"
**What lands:** Naming actual botanicals (nopal cactus, calendula, marshmallow root, kudzu root), cassava syrup/stevia sweetener transparency, non-GMO/vegan/gluten-free/paleo badges.

## General note
Only Card 1's quotes are directly sourced customer language from the site. Cards 2–4 are reasonable inferences from product/FAQ structure — validate with real customer reviews, support tickets, or social comments before using verbatim in ad hooks.
