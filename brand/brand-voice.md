# OLIPOP — Brand Voice

Source: drinkolipop.com brand story/copy patterns. Use for ad copy, captions, product descriptions.

## Tone
Approachable, upbeat, a little nostalgic — scientifically grounded but never clinical or preachy. Sounds like a friend who's genuinely excited about soda tasting good AND doing something, not a supplement label. Confident, not defensive about being "healthy." Playful about flavor, matter-of-fact about function.

Balance: 60% fun/craveable, 40% functional/credible. Never tips fully into either "diet product" territory or "junk food" territory.

## Do
- Lead with taste/craving, back it with function ("tastes like real root beer, plus 9g fiber")
- Use exact numbers (sugar grams, calories, fiber grams) — specificity reads as honest
- Say "prebiotic," "fiber," "gut/digestive health," "microbiome" — precise, not vague wellness-speak
- Nostalgic soda-fountain language: "classic," "old-school flavor," root beer/cola/cream soda callbacks
- First-person-plural warmth: "we," "feed life," inclusive not preachy
- Short, punchy sentences. Confident claims stated plainly, not hedged into mush.
- Structure/function phrasing: "supports digestive health," "supports the microbiome"
- Trademark markers where relevant: "a new kind of soda™," OLISMART®

## Don't
- Don't use diet-culture language: "guilt-free," "cheat," "sinful," "diet soda alternative for weight loss"
- Don't say "probiotic" (it's prebiotic — different thing)
- Don't say "cures," "treats," "fixes," "heals," "reverses"
- Don't use clinical/pharma tone ("clinically proven to eliminate...", "doctor recommended for...")
- Don't be preachy or shame conventional soda drinkers ("stop poisoning yourself with regular soda")
- Don't oversell with vague wellness buzzwords ("detox," "cleanse," "superfood") — none of these appear in OLIPOP's own language
- Don't use hedgy corporate softening ("may potentially help support," "could possibly") — state facts plainly, qualify only where legally required (diabetics/pregnancy/kids → healthcare provider)
- Don't drop the caffeine callout on caffeinated flavors, even in short-form copy

## Sample lines (style reference)
- "The feel good soda."
- "A new kind of soda™ — delicious, refreshing, and actually good for you."
- "9g of prebiotic fiber. 2–5g of sugar. All the flavor of the soda you grew up on."
- "Feed your gut something it'll actually thank you for."
- "Classic Root Beer, reimagined with real ingredients."
